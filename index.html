Snap-Lock Grid Wrap — Device Overview

How the physical measurement device captures residual limb geometry

Cross-Section (Top View)

The strap locks at 4 discrete angular positions. Each snap node clicks into an index position — each click = 5mm of radius.

Side View (Axial Stack)

8–12 bands stack axially. Adjacent band rings are connected by triangle strips to form the lofted 3D surface.

Measurement Protocol
1
Position the snap-lock grid wrap at the first band height on the residual limb. The strap has rigid segments connected by snap joints.
2
The strap self-tightens at 4 angular positions (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°). Each position clicks into a discrete snap index — you hear/feel each click.
3
Read the index number at each angular position. Index 10 at 0° means the limb surface is 50mm from center at the anterior position.
4
Move to the next height and repeat. 8–12 bands cover a full 250mm transtibial stump in ~5 minutes — no plaster, no scanner, no specialist required.
5
Enter the indices here. The software reconstructs the 3D geometry, computes volume and circumferences, and exports a clinical PDF report.
Patent relevance: The snap-lock mechanism (discrete index locking at 4 angular positions) combined with this reconstruction algorithm constitutes a novel measurement-to-geometry pipeline. No existing prosthetic fitting tool captures limb geometry at this resolution without a 3D scanner or plaster cast.
